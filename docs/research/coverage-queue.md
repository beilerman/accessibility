# Broad and deep research queue

Updated November 11, 2026, following Brad's instruction to go broad and deep.
These are research leads and questions. New facts require fresh evidence before
they enter the public catalog.

## Breadth

The current catalog has 102 places. Its categories contain 32 arts venues,
27 parks, 20 museums/attractions, 13 libraries, five transport entries, four
food/drink venues and one shopping destination. Use this distribution to seek
more evidence for everyday activities and less represented types of places.

Cover Northern Kentucky and Greater Cincinnati. Investigate recreation and swimming,
shopping and groceries, food venues, transport and arrival connections, public
services, neighborhood facilities, parks and cultural destinations. Explore new
neighborhoods alongside established visitor destinations.

## Depth

For each venue investigated, pursue specific evidence on the following topics.

- Arrival, parking spaces, drop-off locations and connections from transit.
- Which entrance to use, thresholds, ramps, steps and door controls.
- Routes between floors and areas, elevator locations and lift operation.
- Seating locations, companion arrangements, ticketing and reservations.
- Restroom locations, individual/family facilities and adult changing equipment.
- Quiet areas, noise, flashing lights, sensory equipment and visual preparation.
- Captions, hearing support, interpretation, large print and audio description.
- Rest points, queue arrangements, shade and other conditions affecting stamina.
- Caregiver admission, service-animal arrangements and equipment borrowing.
- Advance notice, collection/return procedures, seasonal limits and closure notices.

Keep each claim within the scope of its source. Record missing evidence and
conflicting statements. A family restroom label alone does not establish its
physical access features.

## Access-photo work

Prioritize pictures of entrances, thresholds, parking-to-door routes, lifts, toilets,
changing facilities, seating and quiet spaces. Include visible barriers when useful.
Inspect every selected image and confirm its venue and reuse licence. Keep full
frames, capture dates where known, attribution and narrow descriptions of what can
be seen. Link inspected official visual guides when their photographs lack a reuse
licence. Continue looking for Northern Kentucky examples.
